Sequential Agenda October - December at HKS

Each session builds on the last. Participants begin with an idea, pressure-test it through planning and prototyping, and receive feedback from peers, attendees, and invited experts.

  1. 01

    October

    Ideation

    Frame the problem, clarify the audience, and shape an initial venture idea with enough definition to develop further.

    Speaker
  2. 02

    October - November

    Funding + Business Plan

    Convert the idea into a business plan with a value proposition, operating logic, funding needs, and a path toward sustainability.

  3. 03

    November

    Feasibility / Prototyping

    Test assumptions, identify constraints, and build a prototype or proof of concept that makes the idea tangible.

  4. 04

    December

    Feedback Sessions

    Present the plan and prototype for structured feedback from fellow participants and experts leading each topic session.

Keynote Speaker

Kathy Pham

Adjunct Lecturer in Public Policy, Harvard Kennedy School

Kathy Pham is a computer scientist, product leader, and founder whose work spans product management, software engineering, AI, ethics, and public-interest technology. At HKS, she created and teaches Product Management and Society, helping students connect technology decisions with public purpose.

For the prototyping session, Kathy offers a practical lens on moving from concept to tested product: defining the user problem, checking feasibility, building responsibly, and shaping prototypes that can earn useful feedback from real communities.

Keynote Speaker

Richard Cavanagh

Adjunct Lecturer in Public Policy, Harvard Kennedy School

Richard E. Cavanagh teaches entrepreneurship and entrepreneurial management at HKS, including courses on entrepreneurship and innovation in the private and social sectors and a business plan workshop. He previously served as Executive Dean of Harvard Kennedy School and as President and CEO of The Conference Board.

For the funding and business plan session, Richard brings direct experience helping students turn early venture concepts into financially literate, strategically grounded plans for private or social-sector enterprises.

Keynote Speaker

Hong Qu

Adjunct Lecturer in Public Policy, Harvard Kennedy School

Hong Qu teaches data visualization at HKS and serves as a research fellow at the Malcolm Wiener Center for Social Policy. He was one of the first engineers on YouTube's startup team, where he helped build core product features including video sharing, channels, and skippable ads.

For the feedback sessions, Hong brings a builder's and storyteller's eye to how teams present evidence, communicate product choices, and use critique to make an idea clearer, more usable, and more compelling.
